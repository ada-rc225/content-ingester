This adaptation re-centres the gradient-descent material on potential energy, equilibrium, and numerical relaxation for second-year mechanical engineering students. The lesson keeps the essential mathematical definitions and algorithmic rules from the source, but it replaces the abstract optimisation framing with a familiar mechanical-engineering narrative that links stationarity to equilibrium and the update rule to a downhill relaxation step.

The structure now follows a clear progression from physical intuition to optimisation notation, then to smoothness and convexity, gradient descent, convergence behaviour, and practical extensions such as momentum and stochastic updates. The Python example is deliberately simple, executable, and aligned with the core update rule so that students can connect the algebra to a working script.
