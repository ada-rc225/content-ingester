Create a long-form teaching resource titled “Eigenvalues, Eigenvectors, and the Power Iteration” for second-year mechanical engineering undergraduates.

The learners have studied vectors, matrices, basic calculus, and basic Python/NumPy, but have not studied iterative eigensolvers.

Use mechanical-engineering examples where pedagogically useful. Treat modal or vibration language as bounded pedagogical framing rather than as an unsupported physical equivalence, and do not omit or replace any required mathematical topic.

Cover all of the following topics:

- the definition of eigenvalues and non-zero eigenvectors;
- the characteristic equation and why iterative matrix-vector methods are useful for larger matrices;
- the orthonormal eigenbasis of a real symmetric matrix;
- the distinction between the eigenvalue of largest magnitude and the largest algebraic eigenvalue;
- the derivation and normalised update rule for power iteration;
- convergence assumptions and the asymptotic factor governed by |lambda_2/lambda_1|;
- sign alternation for a negative dominant eigenvalue;
- failure or limitation cases, including a missing dominant projection, repeated dominant magnitude, a small spectral gap, and a zero matrix-vector product;
- the Rayleigh quotient and eigenpair residual as diagnostics;
- the scope limitation of the symmetric derivation for non-symmetric matrices;
- the connection between the mathematical algorithm and a robust Python/NumPy implementation, including input validation, residual stopping, breakdown handling, and a maximum-iteration fallback.

Include and interpret the worked implementation example

    A = [[4, 1],
         [1, 2]],
    x0 = [1, 1],

whose exact eigenvalues are 3 + sqrt(2) and 3 - sqrt(2). The implementation should estimate the dominant eigenvalue and produce a small residual; either sign of the returned eigenvector is acceptable.

Place exactly three exercises with worked solutions in one final exercise chapter:

1. one conceptual or theoretical-scope exercise;
2. one hand-calculation exercise involving one normalised power-iteration step, a Rayleigh quotient, and a residual;
3. one Python code-diagnostic exercise containing buggy and corrected code.

Write 1500–2000 English prose words.
