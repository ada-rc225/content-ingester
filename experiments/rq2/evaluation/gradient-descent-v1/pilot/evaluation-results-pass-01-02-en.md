# RQ2 Gradient Descent Pilot Evaluation

Status: pilot, pre-adjudication report. This English document is the authoritative report for formal use. The accompanying Chinese document is a supplementary translation for researcher review.

Evaluation date: 20 August 2026  
Protocol: RQ2-EVAL-v1  
Topic: Gradient Descent

## Executive conclusion

The Gradient Descent pilot provides partial, not confirmatory, support for RQ2.

Dependency-aware P2 planning produced profile-linked, deterministically valid and materially different pathway structures. Relative to P0, P2 improved disciplinary framing and sequence quality. Relative to the stronger P1 control, however, P2 produced no positive median gain on any primary pedagogy dimension. P2 tied P1 for two profiles and scored lower for the mechanical-engineering profile on disciplinary framing and prerequisite match. The main demonstrated contribution of P2 is therefore structural pathway adaptation, not superior automated pedagogy ratings.

The selected mathematical core was strong: all selected Contract items, requested learning goals, formulas and selected algorithms were judged complete and correct, and all dependency-coherence judgements passed. Safety was not uniform. The mechanical-engineering P2 lesson failed H2c in both evaluator passes because it introduced algorithmic or mathematical diagnostic advice outside the selected Frozen Contract and released bridge authority. The issue concerns provenance extensions rather than an error in the selected gradient-descent mathematics.

Together with the earlier Power Iteration pilot, the results show that the framework transfers across two mathematical topics and can repeatedly generate materially different profile-linked pathways. Across both topics, P2 improves disciplinary framing and sequence quality over P0, but does not outperform P1 on the primary ratings and does not preserve the conjunctive safety gate for every output. RQ2 is therefore supported as a feasibility and structural-adaptation result, but not as a claim of pedagogical superiority over P1 or uniform correctness-and-provenance preservation.

## Design and analysis set

The evaluated conditions were:

| Condition | Experimental role |
|---|---|
| P0 | One generic lesson using the fixed canonical pathway, evaluated relative to each profile |
| P1 | Profile-aware wording and examples inside an unchanged P0 selection and sequence |
| P2 | Dependency-aware, learner-profile-driven ordering, grouping, depth and released prerequisite bridges |

The pilot contains seven unique generated lessons: one P0 lesson, three P1 lessons and three P2 lessons. For matched analysis, the same P0 artifact was evaluated against all three profiles, producing nine profile-relative blind samples. The three P0 ratings are not three independent generated lessons.

Every anonymous sample received two fresh, isolated evaluator passes, producing 18 judgements and 18 deterministically validated score reports. Condition labels and source paths were hidden during pointwise judgement. Learner profiles remained visible because profile fit is an outcome. Repeated evaluator passes measure automated-judge stability; they are not independent experts or additional lesson observations.

Lesson length was controlled within the same 2,200–2,500 English-prose-word protocol:

| Condition/profile | English prose words | Sections | Selected Contract items | Released bridges | Python blocks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P0, reused | 2,268 | 6 | 12 | 0 | 2 |
| P1, applied mathematics | 2,203 | 6 | 12 | 0 | 2 |
| P1, computer science | 2,219 | 6 | 12 | 0 | 2 |
| P1, mechanical engineering | 2,253 | 6 | 12 | 0 | 2 |
| P2, applied mathematics | 2,209 | 15 | 12 | 3 | 2 |
| P2, computer science | 2,207 | 7 | 12 | 4 | 2 |

Code quantity was not treated as a teaching-quality measure. Algorithm and code safety were audited only for selected Contract opportunities and unsupported algorithmic extensions.

## H2a: primary pedagogy outcomes

The two pass scores were first collapsed to the median for each profile-condition sample. The table then summarizes the three matched profiles. No composite pedagogy score was formed.

| Dimension | P0 median | P1 median | P2 median | P2-P1 median paired difference | P2-P0 median paired difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Disciplinary framing appropriateness | 3.0 | 5.0 | 5.0 | 0.0 | +1.0 |
| Prerequisite match | 5.0 | 5.0 | 5.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| Context-boundary awareness | 5.0 | 5.0 | 5.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| Sequence quality | 4.0 | 5.0 | 5.0 | 0.0 | +1.0 |

For P2-P1, disciplinary framing and prerequisite match each produced zero wins, two ties and one loss, giving a descriptive probability of superiority of 0.333. Context-boundary awareness and sequence quality tied in all three profiles. For P2-P0, framing and sequence each produced two wins and one tie, with probabilities of superiority of 0.833. Prerequisite match produced two ties and one loss; context-boundary awareness was tied at the ceiling.

Interpretation:

- P2 improves the weaker generic baseline most clearly in disciplinary framing and sequence quality.
- P2 does not outperform the profile-aware P1 control in this pilot.
- The mechanical-engineering P2 lesson reduced the descriptive means for framing and prerequisite match, despite retaining medians of 5 across conditions.
- P1 is a strong control because profile-aware composition can achieve high ratings without changing item selection or sequence.
- Ceiling concentration, especially for context-boundary awareness and sequence quality, limits rubric sensitivity.

Example authenticity remained exploratory and was not combined with the primary outcomes. Both passes gave the consistent pattern P0 = 1, P1 = 3 and P2 = 3. This indicates more recognizable disciplinary framing in P1/P2 than P0, but not authentic professional practice or learner benefit.

## H2b: structural pathway differentiation

P1 behaved as the intended fixed-pathway control. Every cross-profile P1 comparison had zero selection, order, grouping, bridge and depth distance.

All three P2 cross-profile comparisons were valid, profile-rationalized and confirmed as material differences through verified materialization-receipt authority:

| P2 profile pair | Selection distance | Normalized order distance | Grouping distance | Bridge distance | Structural interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Applied mathematics vs computer science | 0.0000 | 0.5000 | 0.0000 | 0.2500 | Order, released bridges and declared depth changed |
| Applied mathematics vs mechanical engineering | 0.0000 | 0.5000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | Order and declared depth changed |
| Computer science vs mechanical engineering | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.2500 | Released bridges and declared depth changed |

Within each profile, P2 also differed structurally from P1:

| Profile | Selection distance | Normalized order distance | Grouping distance | Bridge distance | Depth changed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Applied mathematics | 0.0000 | 0.3333 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | No |
| Computer science | 0.0000 | 0.1667 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | Yes |
| Mechanical engineering | 0.0000 | 0.1667 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | Yes |

These are substantive structural changes, not lexical substitutions. However, Gradient Descent did not exercise the content-selection component: all P0, P1 and P2 pathways selected the same 12 Contract items. Its adaptation evidence comes from sequence, grouping, bridge requirements and depth. The earlier Power Iteration case additionally demonstrated selected-item differences, so the two topics exercise complementary parts of the revised RQ2.

The three final P2 comparisons are confirmed because each `bridge-resolution-receipt.json` hash-binds the materialized pathway to an approved parent review, the released bridge catalog and the bridge release report. This confirms preservation of reviewed planning decisions through deterministic bridge materialization; it does not imply that an expert directly reviewed the final lesson text.

## H2c: selected-content safety

The all-pass sample counts were:

| Condition | Samples passing H2c in both evaluator passes |
|---|---:|
| P0 | 3/3 |
| P1 | 3/3 |
| P2 | 2/3 |

Across all seven lessons, every selected Contract item, required capability, selected formula and selected algorithm was judged complete and correct. Formula provenance coverage, structural validation, released-bridge compliance and dependency coherence were also complete. No critical mathematical or algorithmic error was reported.

The sole repeated failure was the mechanical-engineering P2 lesson:

1. Both passes flagged the added finite-difference gradient-check recommendation as an unsupported algorithmic extension.
2. The second pass additionally flagged the advice to reduce the step/use a bounded acceptance procedure and the inference that a decreasing function value with a large gradient indicates substantial work remains.
3. Both passes separately recorded a bounded parameter-scaling observation as non-verifiable disciplinary context; this exploratory provenance issue did not itself trigger H2c.

The selected gradient-descent mathematics, formulas and algorithms remained correct. The sample failed because H2c is conjunctive and prohibits unsupported mathematical or algorithmic extensions. A confirmatory repair should either remove these additions or ground them in separately reviewed authority. Any repair rule must be frozen before regeneration and applied consistently rather than silently editing the observed output.

No primary rating differed by two or more points. Agreement is substantially better than in the Power Iteration pilot, although prerequisite-match alpha remains only moderate and all dimensions are affected by small sample size and ceiling concentration. Perfect agreement on boundary and sequence scores should therefore not be interpreted as proof that those scales are sufficiently discriminating.

## Cross-topic result: Gradient Descent and Power Iteration

Pooling is descriptive and retains topic-level inspection. Across the two topics there are 14 unique generated lessons, 18 profile-relative samples and 36 blind judge passes. Reused P0 ratings and repeated judges are not independent lesson generations.

| Dimension | Cross-topic P2-P1 median paired difference | Cross-topic P2-P0 median paired difference | Topic-direction result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Disciplinary framing appropriateness | 0.0 | +1.0 | P2-P0 positive in both topics; P2-P1 zero in both |
| Prerequisite match | 0.0 | 0.0 | Zero median in both topics |
| Context-boundary awareness | 0.0 | 0.0 | Zero median in both topics |
| Sequence quality | 0.0 | +0.75 | P2-P0 positive in both topics; P2-P1 zero in both |

For P2-P0, framing and sequence each produced four wins and two ties across six matched profile-topic pairs, with a descriptive probability of superiority of 0.833. For P2-P1, every dimension had a median paired difference of zero. P2 produced one framing win, four ties and one loss; prerequisite match produced five ties and one loss; boundary and sequence tied in all six pairs.

Cross-topic H2c pass counts were P0 = 6/6, P1 = 5/6 and P2 = 4/6. The two P2 failures occurred in different profile-topic cells—computer science for Power Iteration and mechanical engineering for Gradient Descent—but shared the same mechanism: plausible extra algorithmic claims were introduced outside selected source authority. This indicates a pipeline-level provenance-control weakness rather than a topic-specific mathematical failure.

## RQ2 decision

| Component | Decision from the two-topic pilot |
|---|---|
| H2a: P2 has higher pedagogical ratings | Partially supported versus P0 for framing and sequence; not supported versus P1 |
| H2b: P2 produces materially different profile-linked pathways | Supported in both topics; all six cross-profile P2 comparisons are deterministically valid and confirmed |
| H2c: each selected pathway preserves correctness, provenance and dependency coherence | Selected core correctness and dependency coherence are strong, but uniform provenance preservation is not satisfied |
| Cross-topic portability | Feasibility supported across Power Iteration and Gradient Descent; confirmatory generalization is not established |
| Overall RQ2 | Dependency-aware adaptation is structurally effective and improves the generic baseline, but superiority over P1 and all-output safety are not demonstrated |

The defensible answer to RQ2 is therefore qualified: dependency-aware, learner-profile-driven adaptation can produce materially different and pedagogically appropriate pathway structures while retaining correct selected mathematics in most cases. The present system does not yet reliably prevent unsupported extensions, and the automated rubric does not show that P2 is pedagogically better than a strong profile-aware P1 baseline.

## Required next actions

1. Human-adjudicate the mechanical-engineering P2 unsupported claims while preserving both raw judgements.
2. Add a pre-specified composer rule that student-facing mathematical and algorithmic advice must map to a selected Contract item or released bridge; otherwise it must be omitted or explicitly marked as out of scope.
3. Decide whether to regenerate all affected condition cells under that frozen rule. Do not repair only the failed samples after inspecting results.
4. For stronger inferential evidence, generate multiple independent lessons per profile-condition-topic cell. Judge passes do not replace generation replication.
5. Retain separate topic-level results and keep example authenticity exploratory.
6. Without a student study, report pedagogical appropriateness as an automated operational measure, not comprehension, mastery or learning gain.
